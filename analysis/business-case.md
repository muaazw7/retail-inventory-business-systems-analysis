# Cost-Benefit and ROI Analysis

## Purpose
This document evaluates the business case for replacing the organization's spreadsheet-based inventory management process with a centralized inventory management and reporting solution.

The analysis considers current process inefficiencies, expected implementation costs, operational benefits, estimated annual savings, return on investment (ROI), and payback period.

## Current-State Challenges
The AS-IS process relies heavily on spreadsheets, manual inventory updates, physical inventory verification, disconnected supplier records, and manually prepared management reports.

These activities create several measurable business costs:

- Employee time spent manually updating inventory records
- Employee time spent identifying low-stock products
- Manual reconciliation of inventory discrepancies
- Time required to prepare recurring management reports
- Increased risk of inventory inaccuracies
- Limited visibility into product and inventory performance
- Potential stockouts caused by delayed replenishment decisions

## Proposed Investment
For the purposes of this simulated business case, the following implementation costs are assumed.

| Cost Category | Estimated Cost |
|---|---:|
| System analysis and design | $4,000 |
| Database development | $5,000 |
| Reporting and dashboard development | $3,000 |
| Testing and implementation | $2,500 |
| User training | $1,500 |
| Initial software and infrastructure costs | $2,000 |
| **Total Estimated Initial Investment** | **$18,000** |

These figures are hypothetical estimates created for project analysis purposes.

## Estimated Operational Benefits
### 1. Reduced Manual Inventory Administration

Assumption:

- 10 hours per week currently spent on manual inventory updates, spreadsheet review, and reconciliation
- Estimated employee cost of $30 per hour
- 50 working weeks per year
- Proposed system reduces this effort by approximately 60%

Annual current cost:

10 hours × $30 × 50 weeks = $15,000

Estimated annual savings:

$15,000 × 60% = **$9,000**

### 2. Reduced Manual Reporting Effort
Assumption:

- 5 hours per week spent collecting data and preparing management reports
- Estimated employee cost of $30 per hour
- 50 working weeks per year
- Automated reporting reduces this effort by approximately 70%

Annual current cost:

5 hours × $30 × 50 weeks = $7,500

Estimated annual savings:

$7,500 × 70% = **$5,250**

### 3. Reduced Inventory Discrepancy Investigation
Assumption:

- Current manual processes require approximately 3 hours per week of discrepancy investigation and correction
- Estimated employee cost of $30 per hour
- 50 working weeks per year
- Centralized transaction records reduce this effort by approximately 50%

Annual current cost:

3 hours × $30 × 50 weeks = $4,500

Estimated annual savings:

$4,500 × 50% = **$2,250**

## Estimated Annual Quantifiable Savings
| Benefit | Estimated Annual Savings |
|---|---:|
| Reduced inventory administration | $9,000 |
| Reduced manual reporting | $5,250 |
| Reduced discrepancy investigation | $2,250 |
| **Total Estimated Annual Savings** | **$16,500** |

## Additional Non-Financial Benefits
The proposed solution is also expected to provide benefits that are more difficult to quantify financially.

These include:

- Improved inventory data accuracy
- Faster identification of low-stock products
- Improved management visibility
- More consistent business processes
- Improved historical data availability
- Reduced dependence on individual spreadsheets
- Improved scalability
- Better support for business intelligence and forecasting
- Stronger auditability of inventory transactions

## Return on Investment
ROI is calculated using:

ROI = (Annual Benefit - Initial Investment) / Initial Investment × 100

Using the estimated first-year figures:

ROI = ($16,500 - $18,000) / $18,000 × 100

ROI = **-8.3% in Year 1**

The first year does not fully recover the implementation investment.

However, assuming similar annual benefits and relatively low ongoing operating costs, the investment becomes financially positive during the second year.

## Payback Period
Payback Period = Initial Investment / Annual Savings

Payback Period = $18,000 / $16,500

Payback Period = approximately **1.09 years**, or approximately **13 months**

## Two-Year Benefit
Estimated gross savings over two years:

$16,500 × 2 = $33,000

Less initial implementation investment:

$33,000 - $18,000 = **$15,000 estimated net benefit**

Two-year ROI:

($33,000 - $18,000) / $18,000 × 100

Two-year ROI = approximately **83.3%**

## Recommendation
Based on the simulated cost-benefit analysis, implementation of the centralized inventory management solution is recommended.

Although the initial implementation cost is not fully recovered during the first year, the estimated payback period is approximately 13 months. The solution is expected to produce positive financial returns during the second year while also providing significant operational benefits through improved inventory accuracy, reduced manual work, standardized reporting, and improved management visibility.

## Assumptions and Limitations
This analysis is based on hypothetical assumptions created for the purposes of demonstrating business systems analysis techniques.

Actual implementation costs, labour costs, efficiency improvements, transaction volumes, software costs, and financial benefits would need to be validated through stakeholder interviews and organizational data before an investment decision is made.
